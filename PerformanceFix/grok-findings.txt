==============================================================================
Verdict
==============================================================================

The followed vehicle’s point patch (dot + line number on source
`transit-vehicles`) is landing inside the display frame it was issued for.

On this phone, in this working tree, Mapbox’s serial GeoJSON queue is not
adding multi-frame delay to that write.

The plan’s own criterion:

  “Expect p50 well over one frame and a long p95 tail. If it comes back
   inside a frame, the diagnosis above is wrong and steps 1–2 should not
   be built.”

For the flat-case path (followed dot / line number) on a lightly tilted
follow, that bar is not met.

The tilted-case path (3D wagons) was measured in a second run at pitch 60.
See “3D wagons” below. Landed wagon patches are often still inside a frame
at p50, but 73 % of wagon writes never get their own load event — wagons
visually update at ~16 Hz while the camera runs at 60 Hz. That is the
tilted-case problem, and it is batching / queue occupancy, not a multi-frame
p50 on the events that do land.

How it was measured
==============================================================================

Instrumentation (gated on `-frameProbe YES`):

  GeoJSONQueueProbe in SwissTransit/App/Diagnostics.swift
  - stamps dataId on GeoJSON writes as kind:CACurrentMediaTime-or-displayTime
  - follow point uses CADisplayLink.targetTimestamp, as the plan specified
    (dataId = "fp:<targetTimestamp>")
  - fleet whole-source rewrite uses CACurrentMediaTime ("fv:<now>")
  - onSourceDataLoaded reports the dataId back; latency = now − stamped time
  - p50/p95 logged once a second and shown on the diagnostics overlay
    as the `queue` row
  - samples persisted on device as
    Library/Application Support/queue-latency.txt
    Library/Application Support/queue-latency.jsonl

Writes stamped:

  fp  followFrame → updateGeoJSONSourceFeatures(transit-vehicles)
      the followed vehicle’s own point, display-rate
  fs  follow-source rebuild / wagon+lamp point patches
      (never fired in this run)
  fv  drawVehicles whole-collection rewrite of transit-vehicles
  vs  whole-collection transit-vehicle-shapes rewrite
      (never fired in this run)

Launch (after a first attempt at zoom 17 left the viewport empty):

  -frameProbe YES
  -showDiagnostics YES
  -startLat 46.94809
  -startLon 7.44744
  -startZoom 14
  -selectVehicle 1
  -openService IC

The app selected IC 8 (Chur → Lausanne), auto-followed, and the follow
camera held zoom 17 with 3D wagons.

Negative fp latency is expected with targetTimestamp: the stamp is the time
the frame is *due*, typically ~16 ms in the future when the display-link
callback runs. Negative means the patch landed before that deadline.
At 60 Hz, p50 of −11 ms vs target ≈ 5–6 ms after enqueue.

==============================================================================
What this means
==============================================================================

1. Flat case (dot + line number) — not the serial-queue story
   The followed point is patched at 60 Hz into the shared fleet source,
   exactly as the plan describes. Those patches come back on
   onSourceDataLoaded inside the same frame. They are not queuing up
   behind whole-fleet parses. Almost every stamp gets its own load event.

   So “the camera and body arrive on time; the dot and number arrive late
   and irregularly” is not what this phone is doing, at least not because
   of GeoJSON queue wait.

2. Fleet rewrites are cheap here
   A full transit-vehicles rewrite lands in a fraction of a millisecond
   p50, a few milliseconds p95. They are not occupying the serial queue
   long enough to bury the follow patches.

3. Occasional tail, not a steady lag
   One 57 ms spike at follow start (camera catch-up). Once settled, worst
   follow-point sample in the rolling window was 29 ms (~2 frames), and
   p95 stayed before the deadline in 142 of 143 seconds. That is not the
   “lags back every frame” picture.

4. 3D wagons — measured in the second run (see below)
   This first follow did not stamp wagon writes (`fs`/`fw` never fired)
   because solids were not forced on. The pitch-60 run did. Short version:
   wagons are on the async path, written ~57 Hz, only ~16 Hz get a load
   event. Landed p50 is often inside a frame; the missing events are the
   finding.

5. Working tree already skips redundant point writes
   followFrame only calls updateGeoJSONSourceFeatures when the feature
   actually changed. For this moving IC that still meant ~60 writes/s,
   so the skip did not hide the load. It would hide it for a standing
   train.

What this means for wagons

1. They are on the async path, at display rate.
   fill-translate slides the 2D footprint. The 16 wagon point features
   are rewritten through updateGeoJSONSource / updateGeoJSONSourceFeatures
   on the follow source. That is exactly the split the plan describes.

2. Mapbox coalesces them.
   The SDK’s own note on updateGeoJSONSourceFeatures: when parses are
   already queued, partial updates apply in batches and only the most
   recent dataId comes back. 73 % of wagon stamps never appear as their
   own onSourceDataLoaded. The camera moves at 60 Hz; the wagons that
   land do so at ~16 Hz. That is “lags back” even when the landed p50
   is inside a frame — the other four writes in each batch never show.

3. Landed p50 is not “well over one frame.”
   Median window p50 is −1.9 ms (before the display deadline). Rolling
   p50 is 6.6 ms. The plan’s numeric bar for “the diagnosis is confirmed”
   is not met on the events that return. p95 is the more honest wagon
   number: 31 ms rolling, and 46 of 129 seconds had p95 over a frame.

4. The rest of the serial queue is busier when solids are up.
   vs (whole-collection 3D shapes for everyone else) always lands
   (2085/2085) but p95 median 16.5 ms, spikes to 85–131 ms.
   fv rolling p50 14.4 / p95 34.1 — much slower than the first run’s
   0.3 / 2.4 ms.
   fp, which was inside a frame when the map was not at 60°, is no
   longer: rolling p50 22.1 / p95 37.0 under this load, 30 of 132
   seconds with p50 over a frame.

5. Thermal.
   `serious` thermal, fps 47, tick cost 17 ms with fleet 13 ms at the
   end of the run. A 60° follow of a 16-wagon IC is not a free probe
   of queue latency alone; the main thread and the renderer are also
   in the way.

==============================================================================
Recommendation (updated after the wagon run)
==============================================================================

Do not start plan step 1 on the strength of “the follow point is late
because of the GeoJSON queue.” On a mild follow that number is inside
a frame. At 60° it degrades, but as a side-effect of load, not as the
primary split.

Step 2 is the one this wagon measurement supports: 3D wagons are on
the async GeoJSON path, they are written ~57 times a second, and only
~16 of those writes become a load event. Putting them on synchronous
`model-translation` (or a ModelSource) is the change that would make
the body land on the same frame as the camera and the footprint.

Do not read landed p50 of 6.6 ms as “wagons are fine.” The missing
73 % of load events is the measurement.
